# The Loudspeaker in the Celery Field

I watch you in a celery field.  
Leaves begin to grow out of the guitar.  
  
Outside the window,  
the [snow](/fragments/F241/) melts little by little,  
  
because once the [Beginning of Spring](/fragments/F271/) has passed,  
the plane takes off.  

You and the children  
who play football on the sports field  
year after year  

have seen, this year,  
the silk scarves left behind  
by the woman who embroidered flowers.  

He wears them.  
So do you.  
  
One summer,  
you pushed open the door of an apartment building   
in an East German city.  

Upstairs, someone from Hunan,  
speaking in the accent of home,  
greeted you in the [stairwell](/fragments/F028/).  
  
This year...  
little by little,  
words can no longer enter  
your glasses or your house.  
  
Yet you remember  
the dust-covered carpet  
at the entrance to the stairwell.  
  
But it is the mole on your face  
that cries like a monkey.  
The tears are swept  
into the washing machine inside the belly  
and the balcony where you stood that day.  
  
Your throat freezes over.  
Your mouth melts.  
  
My intestines...  
sometimes want to [sing](/fragments/A015/)  .  
  
Every six months,  
I see the pigeons in the park  
fly into their cage.  
  
The sky covers them  
and my feet.  
  
...My toes,  
so light they are about  
to lose their sinews.  
  
This story does not really  
need to be [broadcast](/fragments/A0/)  .  
  
Once, whenever you heard a broadcast,  
you wanted to go home.  
  
Now all that remains  
of the broadcast  
is the loudspeaker.  
  
Inside the loudspeaker,  
you pick flowers  
in the celery field.  
  
You come down from his balcony,  
pick up a snowball in the supermarket,  
and place it in an art museum.  
  
The loudspeaker grows louder,  
covering the intestines...  
the throat...  
the mouth...  
  
The mouth is inside the eyes.  
  
“Huh...  
there are no loudspeakers here anymore.”  ƒ
