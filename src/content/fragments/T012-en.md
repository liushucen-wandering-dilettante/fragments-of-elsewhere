# Back to China

Trying to save money made getting back to mainland China difficult.  
  
A month ago, I bought a ticket from Frankfurt to Bucharest, Dubai, New Delhi, and finally Hong Kong. I planned to meet friends in Shenzhen and Guangzhou whom I hadn’t seen for two years.  
  
The flight from Bucharest to Dubai was delayed, so I missed my connection to New Delhi. At Dubai Airport, I bought another ticket to Hong Kong, with a transfer in Haikou. I cleared immigration in Haikou at two in the morning on the fifteenth. At five, when I tried to check in again for Hong Kong, the woman at the counter told me I couldn’t fly there without a permit for travel to Hong Kong and Macao.  
  
So I switched to the train: Haikou–Zhanjiang–Shenzhen.  
  
The hard-seat tickets from Haikou to Zhanjiang were sold out.  
  
Money is far too easy to spend.  
  
A few kind people at the airport helped me book a ride.Thirty yuan.  
  
Everything has become more expensive. At Shaxian Snacks, a Chinese fast-food restaurant, a bowl of wontons cost fifteen yuan. An egg was another two, and some greens another two. Breakfast came to nineteen yuan.  
  
My Chinese SIM card had been suspended. All along the way, I had to ask passengers to share their hotspot so I could make payments.
I still had some renminbi in cash left from before I went abroad, when I exchanged cash for euros.  
  
I could understand what people around me were saying again.  
  
It began to rain. [Subtropical monsoon rain](/fragments/F280/).  
  
I listened to the customers in the breakfast shop talking: civil-service exams, public-sector jobs, school rankings.  
[A woman rode an electric scooter](/fragments/F041/) with an advertisement for lodging hanging from the front—red characters on a yellow background.  
  
From another woman on an electric scooter, I bought a tub of instant noodles for five yuan and two pears. Ten yuan altogether.
At the old railway station, I paid cash to the woman at the ticket counter for [an upper berth in a hard-sleeper carriage](/fragments/F281/). There were only two left.  
  
[A portrait of Mao](/fragments/F024/) hung in the office. [Blue glass](/fragments/F264/). [An electric fan](/fragments/F085/) mounted on the wall.  
  
People [ smoked indoors](/fragments/F275/).   
  
A man was playing Jonathan Lee’s [“The Hill.”](/fragments/A034/)  
  
It took three days.  
  
At last, I boarded the [train](/fragments/F026/) back to the mainland.
