# The Frog and the Fish

A frog jumped down from the clouds and back into the well. All around it were the same scenes it had seen in the sky, [reflected](/fragments/F251/) in the water.  
  
“So there isn’t much difference between being above the clouds and being inside a well. It is only a matter of how [damp](/fragments/F190/) the air is.”  
  
Before it could decide whether it was speaking frog-speech or ghost-talk, the frog chose to release the feeling onto the tip of its tongue. The tongue’s intimate contact with the air briefly allowed it to forget the pain of speechlessness.  
  
Soon, it would see the fish again. In the past, they had often stared blankly at the clouds together from inside the well.  
  
The fish spoke only fish-speech and understood no frog-speech. It was a fish unafraid of death; it simply could not fly. Whenever the well dried up, the fish and the frog would gaze at each other, enjoying their silent companionship in the air.  
  
After the frog left the well, the fish swam into another river and eventually reached the sea. At the time, however, it did not yet know that it was only a freshwater fish.  
  
The sea was far too bitter for it. Every day, it shed tears into the seawater.  
  
At last, they met again inside the well. It was summer now, and the well water was wonderfully cool.  
  
“Glub, glub, glub...” The fish sent [bubbles](/fragments/F284/) rising through the water.  
  
[“Croak...” ](/fragments/A017/)  
  
Hearing such happiness, the frog answered.
