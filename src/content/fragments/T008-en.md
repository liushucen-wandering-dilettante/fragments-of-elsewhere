# South of Red Examination Hall

I return--  
to a little pattering rain,  
to passers-by outside the window  
holding red umbrellas.  
  
Between them and me, watching,  
lie a lens and the rain.  
  
For years, I have been sinking  
into the same [puddle](/fragments/F185/):  
a [frog](/fragments/A017/),  
and mesh shoes about to soak through.  
  
Again, I pass a young person  
with a “rain” written above her head.  
  
She tells me she wants to leave this place,  
like those who, in 2017,  
had just walked out of the Red Examination Hall  
and past the [camphor tree](/fragments/F162/)by the school gate,  
the one that never shed its leaves.  
  
In [water](/fragments/F221/)without end--  
in the flood--  
We will soak our mesh shoes,  
laughing at ourselves:  
  
“Can we go to the bookshop every Tuesday  
and listen to a literary critique?”  
  
Again, you say,  
  
“I could never be a saint.  
All I can do is walk out into the [street](/fragments/F103/)--  
sheltering from rain on hot days,   
seeking the cool on rainy ones.”  
  
And those who once gave records,   
wrote novels,   
and painted school bulletin boards  
have already gone  
into [Shenzhen’s concrete forests](/fragments/F270/),  
where they have put up their houses.
