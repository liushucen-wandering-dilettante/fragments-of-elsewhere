# Chewing
  
“Failure, like time,  
is a kind of experience.”  
Someone says poetry is a shortcut  
into an ideal world.  
Sweet-talked by strangers  
who came before me,  
I entered one [alley](/fragments/F035/)  after another.  
They have already gone halfway,  
or reached the end.  
We young [rabbits](/fragments/F282/)  
chew grass at the mouth of the alley.  
“The world is yours!”  
they shout from the other end.  
  
“Yes, perhaps.  
But—  
it used to be yours.”  
  
Beyond the [spotlight](/fragments/F200/) of success,  
weeds grow thick beneath the stage.  
Someone rolls up their trouser legs,  
ready to smash the old thingsto pieces.  
[Clang, clang, crash](/fragments/A018/).  
  
[Striking marks](/fragments/F283/) remain on the wall.  
I recognize the handwriting.  
Those vanguards  
who no longer need [love](/fragments/A031/)—  
they have won  
a political victory.  
Beneath the flag,  
I try to distinguish  
the conservative   
from the radical.  
  
Yet the rabbits keep eating grass,  
cultivating their stomachs  
into stone.  
  
“You’ve set out  
on a road of no return!”  
Another [echo](/fragments/A029/) rises  
from the mouth of the alley.  
I try to hear  
some hesitation in that voice.  
But time—  
rolling, relentless time—  
and the passing [demolition drills](/fragments/F060/)  
[rumble, rumble](/fragments/A006/),  
drowning out  
the last trace of doubt.  
  
The rabbits emerge  
from their burrows as one,  
waiting for a kind of dream  
or dawn.  
  
Rustle, rustle.
 
Far away,  
people are still rehearsing  
fervor,  
peace,  
or victory.  
  
I   
chew the grass—  
or spit out  
poetry.
