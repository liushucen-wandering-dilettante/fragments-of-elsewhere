#  The Story of a Goldfish

“[Goldfish](/fragments/F146/) for sale! Delicious goldfish, right here!” called a man wheeling a fish tank down the street.  
“Everyone else keeps goldfish to look at. How come yours are supposed to be delicious?” I asked.  
“Things that are beautiful don’t last. Only things that can be eaten do.” He glanced at me and stroked his chin.  
“Really? I would have thought it was the other way around. Things that get eaten are precisely what don’t last. Whoosh—they go into your stomach, and the next day they go down the toilet without so much as a shadow left.”  
He waved his hand, apparently unwilling to say any more. Or perhaps he was simply too lazy to argue with me.  
  
Seeing that he had no interest in continuing the discussion, I turned and walked away.  
Yet something about that night was strange. I couldn’t stop thinking about the goldfish. I dreamed that I was swimming with it.  
  
The next day, I passed the same street corner and saw the man still selling goldfish. I went over and spoke to him.  
“How much is one of your goldfish?”  
“Name your own price.”  
I felt around in my pocket and found that I had exactly five yuan left. I handed it to him.  
“Sold.”  
  
I carried the goldfish home. Since I didn’t have a glass tank, I had no choice but to leave it in the [plastic bag](/fragments/F054/) for the time being.  
I sat there, staring blankly at it.  
“Things that are beautiful don’t last. Only things that can be eaten do.”  
The words echoed inside me.  
  
Ever since the fish entered my home, I would stare at it whenever I had nothing else to do. I stared at it fixedly, almost fiercely. Sometimes I stared so hard that I lost all sense of myself.
Today, while waiting for a bus, I saw a red plastic bag and mistook it for my goldfish. I missed the bus and had to wait another fifteen minutes.  
  
A month later, I seemed to have developed a kind of hysteria. I could barely sleep. All I wanted to do was stare at the goldfish.  
A fit of frenzy seized me.
“Why do you always have to attract my attention? Why do you keep distracting me?” I screamed at it, beside myself with rage.
But it gave me no response whatsoever.
This made me even angrier.
So one night, in a fit of fury, I boiled it.  
  
I cried.  
And I found the peace I had been longing for.  
It was true:  
“Things that are beautiful don’t last. Only things that can be eaten do.”  
Now the goldfish and I have become one.  
I can feel it in my stomach, crushed to pieces and barely alive. The elements my body can absorb have entered my bloodstream.  
From now on, it will endure inside me.
