# Ordinary Maggie
  
She wrote in her notebook:  
But why did she feel the urge to see him?  
He was only an ordinary supermarket cashier in Germany.  
  
Lately, she seemed to have fallen in love with a supermarket cashier. In her notebook, she called him Feifei.  
  
Every Saturday evening, after swimming, she would stop by that supermarket on her way home and check out at the register on the far left. Each time, he was the one behind the counter. He would always look up at her before scanning her groceries.  
  
He worked only three days a week, and at home he had a large black dog. Her name was Maggie—yes, Maggie, after The Gift of the Magi. There was, in fact, a reason she was called Maggie. It was not one of those names people gave their pets on a whim.  
  
Before him, Maggie had had another owner, a woman named Xiao Ou. At the time, Xiao Ou was briefly involved with a man who came to sleep at her place every Saturday. Then she became pregnant by accident. The two of them broke up because of it, but Xiao Ou still decided to have the child. She became a single mother. With no time to care for a beautiful dog like Maggie, she gave her to Feifei.
Feifei was then a third-year history student who repaired bicycles in his spare time. Maggie had been given to him like a gift, and he decided to take responsibility for her. He wanted an undemanding job so that he could spend more time with Maggie.  
  
He studied maritime history, but the more he read, the more absurd it seemed to him: how could people become heroes remembered throughout history simply because they liked sailing? It was terribly unfair.  
  
History is unfair, he thought.  
  
One day, he happened to be walking Maggie when she stopped outside a supermarket and refused to move, no matter how much he coaxed her. He looked through the supermarket’s glass doors and realized that Maggie was staring at a gift box.  
  
Something clicked. He remembered O. Henry’s tender little story, The Gift of the Maggie.  
This dog came to me like a gift, he thought. I’ll call her Maggie.  
  
Later, Feifei dropped out of university. He could see no future in history. What he liked was taking Maggie for [walks](/fragments/V017/). She was the anchor of his life. When he looked at her, it was as though he were looking at a child.  
  
And when he looked at her—  
  
Her [German](/fragments/A001/) was halting and broken. Why did she always come to his register on Saturday evenings, just before the supermarket closed? He was merely curious. He did not think much of it, except for one lingering question:  
  
Why always at this hour?  
  
To her, the way he looked up was a steady signal, no different from the greeting she received each Saturday from the woman checking tickets at the swimming pool.  
  
But something about the way their eyes met yesterday loosened what had been held taut inside her. She began to imagine the life of a supermarket cashier. And she remembered how she had once longed for the ordinary.  

That day, she wrote in her diary:  
Now I [love](/fragments/A031/) only ordinary people. The pursuit of the exceptional makes people strange and immoral.
Morality: an ethics of the ordinary.  
Ordinariness: a virtue.  
  
In the drifting uncertainty of her life in a foreign country, swimming every Saturday and buying enough bread and milk for the coming week just before the supermarket closed had become her anchor.
And the meeting of their eyes was the hook at the end of it.  
  
All these years, the pursuit of being “special” had exhausted her. By now, she had become the “special” one herself. In her hometown, she could tell stories about drifting and travelling through foreign countries. Here, she was a visitor from far away.  
  
But in him, she saw the ordinariness she had forgotten.  
  
In his eyes, she was only another ordinary person waiting in line.  
She was free to be ordinary.  
Now it was her. She began searching her wallet for coins.  
  
The truth was, she knew Feifei. She had been to his home. She had met Maggie. She had even gone for walks with Maggie.  
  
It was only that today, she had thought of Feifei again—of his ordinariness, and her own. So she invented a story about a time before they knew each other.
